## Directly relevant rules

- Article 1: the interpretation applies to disputes over return of bride price given according to custom for the purpose of marriage.
- Article 2: property may not be demanded through marriage; when property is taken in the name of bride price through marriage, a return claim should be supported.
- Article 3: the court considers purpose, local custom, time and method of transfer, value, payer, and recipient when deciding what counts as bride price. Small gifts for special occasions, ordinary spending to maintain the relationship, and other low-value property are excluded.
- Article 4: in an engagement-property dispute, the engaged person and parents who actually paid may be joint plaintiffs; the other engaged person and parents who actually received may be joint defendants.
- Article 5: after registration and cohabitation, return is generally unsupported at divorce. A court may decide whether and how much to return when cohabitation was short and the amount was excessive, considering actual use, dowry, amount, cohabitation, pregnancy or children, fault, local custom, local disposable income, and the payer family's finances.
- Article 6: when the parties did not register but lived together, the court considers actual use, dowry, cohabitation, pregnancy or children, fault, and local custom to determine return and proportion.

## Decision use

Before advising on a bride-price dispute, collect:

1. whether marriage was registered;
2. length and reality of cohabitation;
3. who paid and who received each transfer;
4. amount, time, method, purpose, and local custom;
5. actual use of the money and dowry arrangements;
6. pregnancy or children, fault, and each family's financial position;
7. receipts, messages, bank records, and witness evidence.
