## Verified scope and findings

This theory-based systematic review included 230 studies through December 2016. It classified variables associated with romantic jealousy into personal factors, interpersonal factors, and sociocultural factors. The review describes jealousy as an emotion triggered by a real or perceived threat to a relationship and notes consequences for partners, perceived rivals, and sometimes safety.

The review supports separating three questions: what was observed, what threat was inferred, and what action is safe and proportionate. It does not identify a universal jealousy threshold or establish that any single trait predicts violence or infidelity for an individual.

## Limits

- The review is theory-based and calls for stronger statistical models and clinical formulations.
- The evidence spans varied cultures, measures, and relationship forms; group associations should not be used as personal labels.
- Jealousy can signal insecurity, a real boundary violation, or coercive control; context and behavior must be assessed separately.
