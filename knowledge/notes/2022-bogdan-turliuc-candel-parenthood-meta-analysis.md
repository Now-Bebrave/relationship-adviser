## Verified scope and findings

This meta-analysis included 49 studies, 97 samples of parents, and 9 samples of non-parents. Marital satisfaction showed a medium decrease from pregnancy to 12 months postpartum and a small decrease from 12 to 24 months for both genders. Dyadic analyses found cross-partner associations: a steeper decline in one partner was linked with a steeper decline in the other.

The comparison with non-parents matters: some decline also occurred over a similar period among couples who did not become parents. The transition can add sleep loss, role changes, workload, health demands, and financial pressure, but a decline in satisfaction is not proof that the relationship is failing or that parenthood alone caused it.

## Limits

- This card is based on the abstract and citation metadata, not a full-text review of the meta-analysis.
- Meta-analytic averages do not predict an individual couple's trajectory or establish one universal postpartum timeline.
- Distress, depression, coercion, violence, severe sleep deprivation, or medical symptoms need clinical or safety assessment rather than relationship advice alone.
