## Verified scope and findings

This systematic review integrated dyadic-coping models and reviewed 139 studies (empirical studies). It distinguishes stress communication, positive individual responses, positive conjoint responses, negative individual responses, and negative conjoint responses. Across Western couples, positive individual and conjoint dyadic coping were generally associated with individual and relationship benefits when couples faced general, medical, or mental-health stressors.

The review also warns that the stressor type matters. Dyadic coping is most directly applicable to stressors outside the relationship or shared life stress. For within-relationship stressors such as conflict or infidelity, partners are not automatically each other's coping resource; safety, accountability, and individual support may be needed first.

## Limits

- The empirical review was built from English and German peer-reviewed studies available through 2017 and is not a universal estimate for every culture or relationship form.
- The review integrates multiple models and designs; “positive coping” is not one single intervention with one known effect size.
- Some strategies that look supportive can become overprotection, control, protective buffering, or forced togetherness depending on consent and context.
