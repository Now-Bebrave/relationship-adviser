## Verified scope and findings

This systematic review covered 27 studies. It defines reproductive coercion as behavior interfering with autonomous reproductive decision-making. The reviewed manifestations include birth control sabotage, pressure or coercion to become pregnant, and controlling whether a pregnancy continues or ends.

The review found substantial variation in measurement and prevalence estimates, and much of the evidence was descriptive. Reproductive coercion frequently co-occurred with intimate-partner violence, but it can also occur without physical violence. The review identified evidence gaps in interventions for survivors, perpetrators, and healthcare providers.

Examples described in the review include removing or damaging a condom, refusing condoms after a clear request, interfering with contraception access, lying about fertility or contraception, pressuring pregnancy, and threats tied to reproductive decisions. These behaviors concern autonomy and safety; they are not ordinary “relationship disagreement.”

## Limits

- The review focused primarily on male-partner coercion against women in U.S. research and explicitly notes gaps regarding victims and perpetrators of other sexes.
- Measurement instruments and samples varied; prevalence figures must not be generalized to a specific population or individual.
- This evidence card supports recognition and safety planning, not self-diagnosis or legal classification in a particular jurisdiction.
