## Verified scope

PubMed reports 48 included studies. Sexual communication was positively associated with desire (r = .16), arousal (.21), lubrication (.17), orgasm (.23), erectile function (.19), less pain (.12), and overall sexual function (.35). Associations varied by gender, relationship status, and study region. These are correlations, not proof that communication alone caused better function.

## Safe current use

- Treat sexual communication as a relevant assessment area when users report desire, pleasure, arousal, orgasm, pain, or frequency problems.
- Ask about consent, comfort, health, contraception, preferences, and the ability to discuss dissatisfaction without retaliation.
- Do not assume that communication is the only cause of a sexual difficulty; medical and contextual factors may matter.

## Limits and full-text queue

- The abstract does not establish causal direction.
- Measures and study designs need full-text review.
- Subgroup differences should not be applied to an individual without checking context.
