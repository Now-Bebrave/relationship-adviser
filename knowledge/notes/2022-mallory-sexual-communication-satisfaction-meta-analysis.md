## Verified scope

PubMed reports 93 studies, 209 unique effect sizes, and 38,499 individuals in a current relationship. Sexual communication was positively associated with relationship satisfaction (r = .37) and sexual satisfaction (r = .43). Communication quality showed larger associations than frequency or self-disclosure. Sample relationship status, individualism, gender inequality, and sociosexuality moderated some associations.

## Safe current use

- When sexual dissatisfaction and general relationship dissatisfaction coexist, assess both sexual communication and wider conflict patterns.
- Separate communication frequency, communication quality, disclosure, and comfort rather than asking only whether the couple “talks about sex.”
- Avoid promising that a communication script alone will resolve medical, trauma-related, coercive, or orientation-related issues.

## Limits and full-text queue

- Meta-analytic association does not establish that communication caused satisfaction.
- Cultural moderators warn against applying one script identically across contexts.
- Full text should be reviewed before turning moderator findings into individual predictions.
