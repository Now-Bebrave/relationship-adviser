## Verified scope and findings

This systematic literature review followed PRISMA methods and included 31 full-text articles on the cognitive dimension of unpaid household and childcare work. Mental labor includes anticipating needs, planning, remembering, delegating, monitoring, and making decisions; it is distinct from the physical execution of a task.

Across the reviewed literature, women generally performed a larger share of mental labor, especially in childcare and parenting decisions. The review also describes associations between this unequal cognitive load and stress, lower life and relationship satisfaction, and career consequences.

These are group-level findings about social arrangements, not a rule that women are naturally responsible or that men are naturally unwilling. Use the concepts to identify who carries discovery, planning, execution, follow-up, and consequences in a particular household.

## Limits

- Most samples came from U.S. or European middle-class households and relationships; cultural and economic generalization is limited.
- This card is based on abstract and citation metadata rather than a full-text review of the article itself.
- The review supports recognizing cognitive load and testing fairer arrangements; it does not establish one universal division of labor or diagnose a relationship.
