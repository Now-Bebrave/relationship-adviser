## Verified scope and findings

This scoping review synthesized 209 studies on polyamory and consensual non-monogamy (CNM), defined as romantic, emotional, and/or sexual relationships with multiple people with the knowledge and consent of everyone involved. Research covered relationship outcomes, attitudes, antecedents, relational processes, identity development, family and parenting, and politics or discourse.

The practical boundary is consent and governance, not the number of partners. A relationship can be monogamous or CNM and still contain deception, coercion, unsafe sex, financial control, or violence. Conversely, a CNM arrangement should not be labeled betrayal merely because it differs from monogamy when all affected people have informed and ongoing consent.

## Limits

- This is a scoping review, not a single-effect meta-analysis or a universal success comparison.
- The evidence base includes varied definitions, samples, and relationship structures; it cannot establish one best CNM model.
- Stigma and institutional barriers may affect who is visible in research and how outcomes are reported.
