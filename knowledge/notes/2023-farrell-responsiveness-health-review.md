## Verified scope

This narrative review evaluates perceived partner responsiveness as a specific process within broader relationship quality. It describes responsiveness as feeling understood, validated, and cared for, and reviews associations with physical-health outcomes above and beyond some broader relationship-quality measures. It also states that stronger causal, mechanistic, and generalizable evidence is still needed.

## Safe current use

- Operationalize responsiveness as three observable questions: Did the partner understand the message, acknowledge the experience, and show care through an appropriate response?
- Assess perception and behavior separately. A helpful action can miss the need actually expressed, and a person under stress may interpret ambiguous behavior differently.
- Use responsiveness as a communication target, not as proof that one partner is healthy, loving, or compatible.

## Limits

- This review centers physical health rather than providing a meta-analytic estimate for relationship satisfaction.
- Most cited evidence is observational; responsiveness may reflect both partner behavior and the recipient's interpretation.
- The retracted paper DOI 10.1037/fam0000907 is excluded from this knowledge base and is not evidence for this card.
