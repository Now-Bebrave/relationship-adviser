## Verified scope and findings

The meta-analysis examined attachment anxiety and avoidance in relation to each person's own relationship satisfaction (actor effects) and their partner's satisfaction (partner effects). The indexed abstract and publisher highlights report that both avoidance and anxiety are associated with lower relationship satisfaction and that Actor effects are stronger than partner effects.

This means an individual's attachment-related pattern is more strongly associated with their own appraisal than with the partner's appraisal on average. It does not mean the pattern is solely “inside” one person or that the partner's behavior is irrelevant.

## Safe current use

- Use attachment anxiety and avoidance as tentative relationship-pattern variables rather than fixed identities.
- Ask about concrete behavior from both partners before applying an attachment explanation.
- Do not use an attachment label alone to predict compatibility or recommend ending a relationship.
- Map the current loop instead: trigger, interpretation, proximity or distance behavior, partner response, and short-term consequence.
- Treat attachment scores as dimensions that can vary by relationship and context, not permanent types.

## Limits and full-text queue

- Exact pooled actor and partner effect sizes;
- sample and cultural moderators;
- study-design limitations and publication bias.
