## Verified scope and findings

The abstract reports a longitudinal study of 337 people who had recently been harmed by a relationship partner. Conciliatory gestures, including apologies and offers of compensation, were associated with faster forgiveness and lower reactive anger. The proposed pathway ran through increased perceived relationship value and lower perceived risk of exploitation or repeated harm.

The practical implication is narrow: a repair attempt should contain both recognition of the harm and credible evidence that repeating it is less likely. A request to “move on” without accountability does not meet that standard.

## Limits

- This card is based on the abstract, not a full-text review; methods, measures, and subgroup limits require later verification.
- The study supports an association and a proposed mechanism, not a guarantee that an apology will restore trust.
- Forgiveness is optional. It does not require renewed access, continued cohabitation, sex, money, or relationship continuation.
- Repeated coercion, violence, stalking, retaliation, or severe power imbalance should enter a safety pathway rather than an apology exercise.
