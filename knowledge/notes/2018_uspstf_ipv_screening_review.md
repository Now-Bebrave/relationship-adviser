## Verified scope and findings

The USPSTF evidence review concluded with moderate certainty that screening women of reproductive age for intimate-partner violence and providing or referring those who screen positive to ongoing support services has a moderate net benefit. Available screening instruments can identify IPV. The review did not find that brief interventions or referral information alone were sufficient; evidence for benefit from ongoing support was strongest among pregnant or postpartum women.

The practical implication is safety-first screening in a private setting, followed by a real support connection when risk is identified. Screening is not a demand to disclose, confront a partner, or leave immediately.

## Limits

- This card records the abstract and recommendation summary, not a full review of the underlying evidence report.
- The recommendation is a U.S. preventive-services recommendation and does not replace local clinical, legal, or crisis guidance.
- Screening tools do not predict future violence perfectly and should not be used as a relationship score.
