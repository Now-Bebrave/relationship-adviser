## Verified scope and findings

This PRISMA-based systematic literature review identified 13 empirical articles on trust repair in intimate relationships, particularly after betrayal. The review highlights five recurring themes: proactive transparency, active monitoring, remorse and accountability, shared activities, and clear communication of reasons for the betrayal. Fidelity trust was the most extensively examined form of trust.

The practical implication is conditional: repair requires observable accountability and a bounded way to reduce uncertainty over time. “Active monitoring” must be voluntary, specific, time-limited, and privacy-respecting; it cannot mean forced passwords, perpetual location tracking, or punishment for asking to stop.

## Limits

- This card is based on the abstract and citation metadata, not a full-text review of all 13 articles.
- The review identifies themes in a small and methodologically limited evidence base; it does not establish a guaranteed repair protocol.
- Repair is optional. A harmed person can decline reconciliation, continued access, cohabitation, sex, or financial entanglement.
