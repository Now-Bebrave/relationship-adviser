## Findings available from the abstract

The review included 86 codable reports, 117 studies, and more than 500 effect sizes. Experimental studies reported effects of d = .30–.36 for relationship quality and d = .43–.45 for communication skills. Moderate-dose programs had larger effects than low-dose programs.

## Practical use

- Structured relationship education can be offered as a concrete option before or during marriage, especially when a couple needs repeatable communication and conflict skills.
- Recommend programs with enough practice and follow-through rather than a one-off lecture.
- Treat education as skill development, not proof that a couple is compatible or that serious coercion and violence can be solved by communication training.

## Limits

- The samples lacked enough racial, ethnic, and economic diversity for reliable conclusions about disadvantaged couples.
- Stability and aggression outcomes were rarely studied.
- Published communication-skill studies showed larger follow-up effects than unpublished studies, suggesting possible publication bias.
