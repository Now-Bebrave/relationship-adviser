## Verified scope and findings

This open-access mixed-methods study analyzed 229 adults in long-term relationships. Participants described 17 strategies for days when desire was out of sync, grouped into disengagement, communication, solo activity, partnered non-sexual or sexual activity, and having sex anyway. Partnered strategies and strategies participants rated as helpful were associated with higher sexual and relationship satisfaction, while the study found no clear association with sexual desire itself.

Communication, alternative shared activities, physical closeness without sex, and mutually agreed alternative sexual activity appeared in the reported strategies. “Having sex anyway” also appeared, but some participants described obligation, relationship fear, or ignoring their own lack of desire. The data therefore cannot support treating unwanted sex as a recommended solution.

## Limits

- The sample was recruited mainly through social networks, was mostly White and North American, and represented individuals rather than matched couples.
- The design is mixed-methods and largely cross-sectional; associations do not show that a particular strategy causes satisfaction.
- The study does not establish a universal frequency or a gender rule for desire. Desire discrepancy can be a normal fluctuation, a medical issue, a stress response, or a relationship problem.
