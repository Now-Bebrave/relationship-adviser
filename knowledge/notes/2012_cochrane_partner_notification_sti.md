## Verified scope and findings

This Cochrane review included 26 trials with 17,578 participants and compared partner-notification strategies for STIs, including HIV. Strategies included simple or enhanced patient referral, expedited partner therapy, provider referral, and contract referral. The review found moderate-quality evidence that expedited partner therapy was better than simple patient referral for preventing reinfection in combined trials of some STIs causing urethritis or cervicitis, but it was not superior to enhanced patient referral. Evidence was insufficient to identify one optimal strategy for every STI or HIV situation.

The practical implication is to involve a qualified clinic in choosing the notification route. The person receiving a result does not need to improvise medical advice, disclose in an unsafe setting, or confront a partner alone.

## Limits

- The review's search and evidence base are historical and do not replace current local clinical guidance.
- Effects differed by infection and notification strategy; no single method should be generalized to all cases.
- Notification, testing, treatment, and legal duties depend on the infection and jurisdiction. This card is not medical or legal advice for a specific case.
