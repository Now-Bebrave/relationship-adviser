## Verified scope and findings

This cross-sectional open-access study compared 71 people who had broken up within the prior six months with 46 people currently in relationships. The breakup group had higher depression-symptom scores; 26.8% reported symptoms in the mild-to-severe range. “Sudden loss” and “lack of positive affect” were associated with symptom severity.

The practical use is triage, not diagnosis: a breakup can disrupt sleep, mood, interest, and hope enough to deserve active monitoring. Distress after a breakup is not proof of a disorder, and a group percentage cannot predict one person's outcome.

## Limits

- Participants were 18–26, Western, heterosexual, Dutch-speaking, and recruited in one study context; generalization is limited.
- The design was cross-sectional, so it cannot establish that the breakup caused later depression or identify an individual clinical threshold.
- The paper is not a treatment trial. Persistent or dangerous symptoms require qualified mental-health assessment.
