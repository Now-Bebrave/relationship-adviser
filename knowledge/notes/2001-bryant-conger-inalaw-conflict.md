## Verified scope and findings

This prospective study examined couples married for nearly two decades. Discord with in-laws predicted later perceptions of marital success for wives, with related findings for husbands' conflict with fathers-in-law. The study also tested the reverse direction and found a more limited reverse association. The authors describe extended-family conflict as a factor that can erode marital stability, satisfaction, and commitment over time.

The practical implication is not that in-laws are inherently harmful. It is that repeated boundary conflict should be treated as a couple-level governance problem: the partners need an agreed rule, an enforcement plan, and a way to prevent one partner from being left alone to confront their own family.

## Limits

- This card is based on the abstract and citation metadata, not a full-text review.
- The sample and measures reflect one historical U.S. study; it does not establish a universal cultural rule or a simple causal effect.
- Conflict with family can coexist with support and affection; classify the specific behavior and impact rather than assigning a global label to a family.
