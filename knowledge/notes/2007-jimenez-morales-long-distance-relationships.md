## Verified scope and findings

Two studies compared long-distance dating relationships (LDDRs) with geographically close dating relationships. LDDR partners showed more idealization and communication satisfaction and could show greater stability while geographically separated. However, LDDRs were more likely to end after becoming geographically close; longer absences between visits and extreme idealization during separation predicted instability after reunion.

The practical implication is to treat reunion as a new evidence phase. Video calls and messages can maintain connection, but they cannot fully test daily routines, conflict, money, sex, family boundaries, or how each person handles ordinary disappointment.

## Limits

- This card is based on an abstract summary of two studies, not a full-text review.
- The findings concern dating relationships and cannot be assumed to apply to every long-distance marriage, culture, or relationship form.
- Idealization is not automatically false or harmful; the risk concerns extreme, untested assumptions that are not revised after real contact.
