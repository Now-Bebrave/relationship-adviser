# Structured relationship education playbook

## Evidence base

Hawkins et al. (2008), DOI 10.1037/a0012584, synthesized 117 studies. The abstract reports small-to-moderate effects on relationship quality and communication skills, with larger effects for moderate-dose than low-dose programs. Diversity and outcomes such as stability and aggression were important gaps.

## When to recommend it

- a couple wants repeatable conflict and communication skills;
- the relationship is serious enough to justify several sessions of practice;
- both people can participate voluntarily;
- the problem is not immediate coercion, violence, stalking, or severe untreated crisis.

## Practical selection checklist

1. Choose a program with multiple sessions, practice, homework, and review.
2. Require concrete skills: softened start-up, listening, repair, problem definition, negotiation, and follow-up.
3. Check whether the material fits the couple's language, culture, finances, relationship form, and actual conflict level.
4. Define one measurable target before starting, such as reducing unresolved weekly arguments or completing a monthly financial meeting.
5. Review after the agreed dose; continue only if behavior changes outside the sessions.

## Stop or switch conditions

- one person uses the program to control or diagnose the other;
- aggression, fear, or retaliation makes joint exercises unsafe;
- sessions produce insight but no observable behavior change after the agreed review period;
- the provider promises guaranteed relationship preservation.
