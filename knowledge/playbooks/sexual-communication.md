# Sexual communication playbook

## Evidence base

- Mallory, Stanton & Handy (2019), DOI 10.1080/00224499.2019.1568375.
- Mallory (2022), DOI 10.1037/fam0000946.

Both sources are meta-analyses. Their findings describe associations across samples and do not prove that a single conversation will cause improvement.

## Decision sequence

1. Check consent, coercion, pain, bleeding, medication effects, pregnancy risk, infection risk, trauma triggers, and whether either person feels unsafe.
2. Define the concrete issue: desire, initiation, frequency, pleasure, arousal, orgasm, erection, lubrication, pain, contraception, exclusivity, or boundaries.
3. Separate communication quality from frequency. A frequent conversation can still be blaming, vague, or unsafe.
4. Choose one small request that can be tried and reviewed rather than trying to solve the entire sexual relationship at once.
5. Set a calm review time outside sexual activity.

## Suggested script

> 我想和你一起把我们的亲密体验变得更舒服。不是要给你打分，也不是要求你现在答应什么。我想先听听你喜欢什么、哪里不舒服，以及有什么明确不想做的。我们先选一件双方都愿意尝试的小事，之后再复盘。

## Reaction branches

| Reaction | Response and next step |
|---|---|
| Willing to talk | Each person states one preference, one boundary, and one small experiment; set a review time. |
| Embarrassed or vague | Use written options or a 0–10 comfort scale; do not pressure for immediate disclosure. |
| Defensive | Restate that the discussion is about shared comfort, give one observable example, and pause if blame escalates. |
| Refuses any discussion | Do not push in the moment; ask for a specific later time. If refusal persists, treat the inability to negotiate intimacy as a relationship decision variable. |
| Pain, coercion, or health concern | Stop the relevant activity and use appropriate medical, sexual-health, or support resources before experimentation. |

## Stop conditions

- consent is absent, withdrawn, or obtained through pressure;
- pain, bleeding, acute health risk, or trauma activation occurs;
- threats, punishment, humiliation, recording, or privacy violations occur;
- one person repeatedly refuses both the topic and any alternative time to discuss it.
